Best Overall: SVM (94.4% Accuracy)

The original SVM with sigmoid kernel achieved the highest test accuracy, demonstrating that simpler models can excel with proper tuning.

Best ROC-AUC: XGBoost & LightGBM (95.8%)

Advanced gradient boosting models excelled at class separation, critical for mission-critical predictions.

Key Data Science Lesson

Different random_state values (2 vs 42) created different test sets. With only 18 test samples, this explains the accuracy gap. See Investigation →

Simpler Can Be Better

SVM excels on small datasets (n=90) due to the bias-variance tradeoff. Kernel methods handle high-dimensional sparse data efficiently.

Important Statistical Caveat

The 94.4% accuracy is based on only 18 test samples. A single misclassification changes accuracy by 5.6%. These results should be interpreted with caution:

  • Confidence intervals are wide due to small sample size
  • Performance may vary significantly on new data
  • Cross-validation scores provide more robust estimates
  • Production deployment would require larger validation set

Why SVM (94.4%) Outperformed Advanced Models (83.3%)

Different Test Sets Used!

Original models: random_state=2
Advanced models: random_state=42
With only 18 test samples, different splits can cause 10%+ accuracy swings.

Feature Differences

Original: 72 one-hot features (incl. sparse Serial columns)
Advanced: 57 engineered features

Class Balancing

Original: No SMOTE
Advanced: SMOTE (72→96 samples)

Small Dataset

Only 90 samples total. Kernel methods like SVM often excel on small, high-dimensional data.

Key Lesson

More complexity ≠ better performance. The Bias-Variance tradeoff favors simpler models on small datasets.

What We Still Learned

Despite the accuracy difference, SHAP analysis revealed the same key predictors: LandingPadUsed, ReusedCount, and GridFins/Legs are critical for landing success regardless of which model is used.

In-depth exploration of factors affecting Falcon 9 landing success

Flight Number vs. Launch Site Analysis

Key Finding: Learning Curve Effect

The earliest flights all failed while the latest flights all succeeded. This demonstrates SpaceX's operational learning curve.

Site-Specific Patterns

  • CCAFS SLC 40: Approximately half of all launches, with higher failure rate when flight numbers were low
  • VAFB SLC 4E and KSC LC 39A: Higher success rates overall

Actionable Insight

Each new launch has a higher rate of success. Operational experience significantly improves outcomes.

Payload Mass vs. Launch Site Analysis

Weight Impact

For every launch site, higher payload mass correlates with higher success rates. Most launches with payload mass over 7,000 kg were successful.

KSC LC-39A Excellence

This site has 100% success rate for payload mass under 5,500 kg - unique among all sites.

VAFB Limitations

No rockets launched for heavy payloads (>10,000 kg) from VAFB-SLC, possibly due to trajectory or infrastructure constraints.

Success Rate Yearly Trend

2013-2017: The Rise

Steady improvement as SpaceX refined landing technology. Success rate climbed from ~40% to ~80%.

2014-2015: R&D Phase

Flat success rate during intensive research and development. SpaceX was testing, failing, and learning.

2017-2018: The Dip

IMPORTANT: This decrease was NOT random!

Causes:

  • More difficult mission profiles attempted
  • Intentional expendable launches (no landing attempted)
  • Transition to upgraded Block 5 hardware with initial technical issues

This is a classic pattern during major technology upgrades.

2018-2020: Recovery

Block 5 boosters proved reliable after initial growing pains. Success rate recovered and exceeded previous highs.

Machine Learning Analysis Deep Dive

Why SVM Achieved 94.4% Accuracy

  • Kernel Trick: Finds non-linear patterns in data
  • Small Dataset Friendly: Works well with ~90 samples
  • Regularization: Robust to overfitting

Critical: False Positives Issue

The confusion matrix reveals that FALSE POSITIVES are the primary error type.

What this means: Model predicts SUCCESS when actual outcome was FAILURE

Business Impact: Could lead to underestimating launch costs by assuming reusability

Mitigation: Use conservative estimates when model predicts success

The Ensemble Paradox

Advanced models (XGBoost, LightGBM) achieved only 83.3% - WORSE than simple SVM.

Why? On small datasets, complex ensemble methods can overfit to training noise.

Key Lesson: More complex does not equal better. Model selection must match data characteristics.

Geospatial Analysis: Why These Locations?

Equator Proximity

The Earth rotates at 1,670 km/hour at the equator. Rockets inherit this speed, getting a "free" velocity boost.

Result: Up to 10% fuel savings = more payload capacity

Coastal Safety

All launch sites are near coastlines. If something goes wrong, debris falls into the ocean rather than populated areas.

A failed rocket can travel 15-20 km in seconds.

Conclusions

Key Conclusions & Recommendations

Actionable insights for stakeholders in the commercial space industry

Technical

Landing success is primarily determined by mission physics (orbit type, payload mass) rather than random factors.

Evidence: GTO missions show 26% lower success rate than LEO missions due to fuel budget constraints
SpaceX can accurately predict recovery probability before launch and plan accordingly

Operational

Flight experience and hardware iteration drive continuous improvement.

Evidence: Success rate improved from 0% to 100% over 7 years; Block 5 achieves near-perfect reliability
Competitors face a significant experience gap that cannot be closed quickly

Geographic

Launch site selection involves complex trade-offs between physics, safety, and customer requirements.

Evidence: Florida sites benefit from ~1,470 km/h rotational velocity boost; VAFB necessary for polar orbits
Multi-site capability provides operational flexibility and mission optimization

Predictive

Machine learning can predict landing outcomes with high accuracy, but small sample sizes warrant caution.

Evidence: 94.4% test accuracy with SVM/Decision Tree, but only 18 test samples
Models are useful for planning but should be validated with larger datasets

Economic

Reusability has fundamentally disrupted the launch industry economics.

Evidence: $62M booster cost recovery per landing; ~50% of price advantage comes from reusability
Traditional launch providers must adapt or face continued market share loss

Recommendations

Prioritize LEO and ISS missions for new booster qualification flights

Higher success probability builds flight heritage safely before risking boosters on GTO missions.

Continue Block 5 optimization rather than introducing Block 6

Data shows Block 5 achieving near-perfect reliability; stability may be more valuable than incremental improvements.

Implement ML-based pre-launch landing probability assessments

94.4% accuracy models can inform fuel loading, landing site selection, and customer communications.

Focus on differentiation rather than reusability replication

SpaceX's 10-year data advantage is difficult to overcome; alternative competitive strategies may be more viable.

Consider partnerships for landing technology licensing

Building in-house capability requires significant R&D investment and flight heritage accumulation.

Evaluate mission requirements carefully when selecting providers

SpaceX's reusability advantage is most significant for LEO missions; GTO missions may have smaller cost differential.

Consider launch cadence requirements alongside cost

SpaceX's high launch rate enables schedule flexibility that may have operational value beyond cost savings.

Validate models on larger datasets before production deployment

18-sample test sets produce wide confidence intervals; real-world performance may differ.

Consider ensemble methods and additional feature engineering

Weather conditions, sea state, time-of-day, and other factors may improve prediction accuracy.

Explore time-series approaches for trend prediction

Success rate improvement over time suggests non-stationary data that may benefit from temporal modeling.

The Bottom Line

This analysis demonstrates that Falcon 9 landing success is predictable and improvable through data-driven engineering. The combination of mission physics, operational experience, and machine learning creates a powerful framework for understanding and optimizing rocket reusability - the key to making humanity a multi-planetary species.
